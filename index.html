05

After You Form

Forming the company is the starting line. Here's what comes next — taxes front and center. This section is educational: it explains the steps, it is not tax advice, and a CPA is worth the money before your first deadline.

▸ Get your EIN

Your federal tax ID from the IRS — free, online, and needed for your business bank account, taxes, and hiring. A company without an EIN runs on your personal Social Security number, which defeats the point of forming.

▸ Decide the S-corp election — on a deadline

An LLC (or C-Corp) can elect S-corp tax treatment with Form 2553, but it must be filed within 2 months and 15 days of the start of your tax year. Miss the window and you generally wait until next year — mark this date the week you form.

▸ Quarterly estimated taxes

There's no employer withholding your taxes anymore. Most owners pay estimated taxes four times a year to the IRS and their state — skip them and the bill arrives with penalties attached.

▸ Sales-tax registration

If you sell taxable goods or services, register with your state to collect and remit sales tax before your first sale. The rules are state-by-state — check yours, don't guess.

▸ Bookkeeping from day one

A separate business bank account and simple bookkeeping from the first dollar. Clean books are what make tax time survivable — and they're half of what a business is worth if you ever sell it.

▸ Annual filings & state fees

Most states want an annual report (and a fee or franchise tax) to keep your company in good standing. Miss them long enough and the state can dissolve the company you just paid to form.

▸ Licenses & permits

Your city, county, or trade may require a license before you can legally take the first job. Your corporate kit and formation details make these applications faster to complete.

▸ Keep the books real

Minutes, membership/share records, and contracts in your Corporate Kit Book aren't decoration — they're the paper trail that keeps your company treated as a company.
